## Title
The Isdal Woman: The Woman Who Erased Her Own Identity

## Description
In November 1970, hikers found a burned body in Norway's Isdalen valley — the "Ice Valley" above Bergen. Someone had cut the labels off her clothes, her belongings and her suitcases. She had checked into hotels under at least eight false names, carried a notebook written in code, and left no document, witness or family that could say who she was.

This film rebuilds the case file piece by piece: the discovery, what the autopsy established (and deliberately did not), the suitcases at Bergen railway station, Room 407, the eight aliases, the decoded notebook, the 1971 verdict of probable suicide — and the three theories that still divide everyone who studies the file. Suicide? A second person? Espionage? Each is weighed against the evidence. None is adopted.

In 2016 the case was reopened. Isotope analysis now points to a birth around 1930, in or near Nuremberg, with a childhood in France or along its border. Her DNA tells us more. Her name remains unknown.

Researched against primary police reporting, BBC/NRK's Death in Ice Valley, and contemporary Norwegian coverage. Every visual is a labelled schematic reconstruction — no archival photographs, no AI imagery, no crime-scene depictions. Full source list: SOURCES.md in this repository.
