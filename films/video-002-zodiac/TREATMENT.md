# TREATMENT — The Zodiac Killer: The Ciphers, the Letters, and the Killer Nobody Could Identify

VIDEO 002 · Lemo-Opuscar · Style: **Halftone Dossier** · Target runtime 15–18 min (narration ≈ 2,100–2,250 words + pauses ≈ 16:00–16:40)

## 1. Candidate structures

1. **The dossier countdown** — read the file exhibit by exhibit in strict archival order (title → attacks → letters → ciphers → suspects → verdict "OPEN"). Serious, legible, but risks feeling like a list.
2. **The central-question spine** — one question drives the file: *how can a killer leave this much material and stay unknown?* Every exhibit is scored against that question; the final page answers it with an evidence audit (CONFIRMED / DISPUTED / UNKNOWN). Chosen: it turns a chronology into an argument, matches the mission statement, and gives the ending a real payoff without fabricating a solution.
3. **The cipher-as-narrator** — the ciphers themselves cut the film (each chapter opens on a code that must be broken to continue). Visually strong, but it overweights the ciphers against victims, suspects and the investigation.

**Chosen: #2**, told through the dossier grammar of the style: exhibits, stamps, redactions, an evidence board, and a closing file-drawer.

## 2. Logline and arc

A nameless killer mailed newspapers cryptograms and confessions, then vanished inside his own evidence. Setup: four attacks and a torrent of mail (1968–69). Turn: two of his own ciphers are cracked — in 1969 and, after 51 years, in 2020 — and neither contains his name. Ending: an evidence audit of what is confirmed, disputed and unknown; the file is stamped **CASE REMAINS OPEN** and slides into the drawer.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Learn from*: Mindhunter's restraint in recreations; David Oranchak's "Let's Crack Zodiac" clarity in cipher explanation; 1960s Bay Area front-page typography (heroic black serif heads, tight columns).
- *Do not take*: gore, dramatized recreations of killings, real documents/photos, suspect names as accusations, any claim not in the sources.

## 4. Facts locked (from research; do not drift)

- Confirmed attacks: Lake Herman Rd, Benicia (Dec 20 1968 — David Faraday, Betty Lou Jensen killed); Blue Rock Springs, Vallejo (July 4 1969 — Darlene Ferrin killed, Michael Mageau wounded/survived); Lake Berryessa (Sept 27 1969 — Cecelia Shepard killed, Bryan Hartnell wounded/survived; hooded attacker); Presidio Heights, SF (Oct 11 1969 — cab driver Paul Stine killed). 5 killed, 2 wounded survivors; killer claimed 37 — never substantiated.
- Letters: Aug 1 1969 ×3 (Chronicle / Examiner / Times-Herald) each with ⅓ of the 408; name "Zodiac" debuted in the Aug 4 letter ("This is the Zodiac speaking"); Oct 13 1969 letter carried a piece of Stine's shirt; Halloween card Oct 1970; last broadly accepted letter Jan 29 1974 ("Exorcist").
- 408: homophonic substitution; solved Aug 1969 by Donald & Bettye Harden (Salinas, no computer); plaintext includes "I like killing because it is so much fun"; ends refusing his name; final 18 characters never conclusively resolved.
- 340: mailed Nov 8 1969 to the Chronicle; solved Dec 5 2020 by David Oranchak (US), Sam Blake (Australia), Jarl Van Eycke (Belgium); FBI confirmed; solution had a transposition layer (period-19 reading order) + homophones; text: "I hope you are having lots of fun in trying to catch me…" — no name, no crime details.
- Unsolved: Z-13 (Apr 20 1970, "My name is —", 13 symbols; too short to verify) and Z-32 (Jun 26 1970, 32 symbols + Phillips 66 map, Mt. Diablo; "the bomb" never found).
- Suspects: thousands named; Arthur Leigh Allen never charged (searches 1991; died Aug 26 1992; DNA on confirmed-letter stamp reported to have excluded him — SFPD statements); 2021 "Case Breakers"/Gary Francis Poste claim publicly rejected by FBI/authorities; case remains open.
- Challenges: 1969 forensics (no DNA profiling, no CCTV, no databases), dispatch miscommunication after the Stine murder, unmatched prints and handwriting, degraded DNA insufficient for genealogy, confirmation bias.
- Riverside (Cheri Jo Bates, 1966) = disputed, presented as disputed only.
