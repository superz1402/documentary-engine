## Description

In 1969, a killer in Northern California began mailing newspapers his own hand-made ciphers, taunting letters, and proof of his crimes — and signed them with a crosshair.

He left behind almost everything an investigation could ask for: authenticated letters, fingerprints, handwriting on hundreds of envelopes, DNA on the stamps and seals, and four of his own secret codes. Two of those ciphers were eventually broken — one in 1969 by a schoolteacher at his kitchen table, one in 2020 by a team of amateur code-breakers after 51 years. Neither contained his name.

This documentary reads the case file exhibit by exhibit: the four confirmed attacks of 1968–69 and the people killed and wounded; the letters that named the Zodiac; how the 408 and 340 ciphers actually worked and what their solutions really said; the two short ciphers that remain unresolved; the suspects who were investigated and why none was ever charged; and an honest audit of what the evidence proves, what is disputed, and what remains unknown.

No reconstruction pretends to be archive. No claim outruns its source. The case remains open.

Notes on accuracy:
- Five deaths are confirmed; the killer's claim of 37 victims is not supported by evidence.
- The 408 (1969) and 340 (2020) ciphers are solved; the 13-character "My Name Is" (1970) and 32-character map cipher (1970) remain unresolved.
- Cipher grids, letters and newspaper pages shown are labeled illustrative reconstructions, not archival scans.
- The 1966 Riverside connection and the final 1974 letters are presented as disputed, following the investigating agencies.
